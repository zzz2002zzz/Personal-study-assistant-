# Agile Project Management

## Origins: The Agile Manifesto (2001)
Agile was formalized around four core values:
1. Individuals and interactions **over** processes and tools
2. Working software **over** comprehensive documentation
3. Customer collaboration **over** contract negotiation
4. Responding to change **over** following a plan

Note: the items on the right still have value — Agile just prioritizes the items on the left.

## Twelve Agile Principles (Key Ones for Exams)
- Highest priority is customer satisfaction through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
- Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
- Deliver working software frequently (weeks rather than months).
- Business people and developers must work together daily.
- Build projects around motivated individuals; give them the environment and support they need.
- Working software is the primary measure of progress.
- Simplicity — maximizing the amount of work *not* done — is essential.
- The best architectures/requirements/designs emerge from self-organizing teams.

## Scrum Framework
The most widely used Agile framework.

**Roles:**
- **Product Owner**: owns and prioritizes the product backlog, represents stakeholder/business interests.
- **Scrum Master**: facilitates the process, removes impediments, shields the team from disruption; not a traditional "manager."
- **Development Team**: self-organizing, cross-functional group that builds the increment.

**Artifacts:**
- **Product Backlog**: prioritized list of everything that might be needed in the product.
- **Sprint Backlog**: subset of the product backlog selected for the current sprint, plus a plan for delivering it.
- **Increment**: the sum of all completed product backlog items at the end of a sprint, meeting the "Definition of Done."

**Events:**
- **Sprint**: a fixed-length iteration, typically 1–4 weeks, producing a potentially shippable increment.
- **Sprint Planning**: team selects backlog items and plans the sprint.
- **Daily Scrum (Stand-up)**: 15-minute daily sync (what I did, what I'll do, blockers).
- **Sprint Review**: demo the increment to stakeholders, gather feedback.
- **Sprint Retrospective**: team reflects on process and identifies improvements for the next sprint.

## Kanban
A visual workflow management method using a **Kanban board** with columns (e.g. To Do / In Progress / Done). Key principles: visualize work, limit **Work in Progress (WIP)**, manage flow, make process policies explicit, and improve collaboratively. Unlike Scrum, Kanban has no fixed iterations — it's a continuous flow system.

## Estimation Techniques
- **Story Points**: relative sizing of effort/complexity/uncertainty (often Fibonacci-like sequence: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13).
- **Planning Poker**: team members privately estimate story points, then reveal simultaneously to surface and discuss differences in understanding.
- **Velocity**: the average number of story points a team completes per sprint, used to forecast future capacity.

## Agile vs Waterfall (Predictive) Comparison
| Aspect | Waterfall | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Fixed upfront | Evolve iteratively |
| Delivery | One deliverable at end | Frequent incremental releases |
| Change | Discouraged/costly late | Welcomed throughout |
| Customer involvement | Mainly at start/end | Continuous |
| Documentation | Heavy, formal | Lightweight, "just enough" |
| Best suited for | Stable, well-understood requirements (e.g. construction, regulated systems) | Uncertain/evolving requirements (e.g. new software products) |

## Common Exam Angles
- Explain a Scrum role/artifact/event and its purpose.
- Compare Agile vs Waterfall for a given project scenario and justify which is more suitable.
- Calculate/interpret velocity given past sprint data.
- Distinguish Scrum from Kanban.
